# Project Supplementary Student Regulations
## Attendance Policy

> **Corpus status:** Synthetic project material created for controlled RuleGuard testing. It is not an official Medi-Caps University policy.

### 1. Attendance Recording

Attendance is recorded for scheduled teaching activities in accordance with the attendance procedure adopted by the relevant department. Students are responsible for being present at the scheduled start of the class or activity and for following the attendance-marking procedure.

Attendance should reflect actual participation. A student should not ask another student to mark attendance on the student's behalf. Any dispute concerning an attendance entry should be raised through the department's prescribed process.

### 2. Attendance Calculation

Attendance percentage is calculated from the sessions counted as attended and the sessions counted as scheduled for the relevant course or academic period. The calculation may differ between theory courses, practical courses, workshops, and other components where the programme rules prescribe a separate method.

Approved academic activities may be treated according to the relevant attendance procedure. Merely being physically present on campus does not automatically count as attendance in a particular class.

### 3. Minimum Attendance for Examination Eligibility

Students must maintain at least **75% attendance in a course** to be eligible to appear for the corresponding end-semester examination, unless a specific approved exception applies.

A student below the required percentage should not assume that the shortfall will be automatically waived. Any condonation, exemption, or special consideration must be supported by the applicable procedure.

### 4. Attendance Shortage

A student who expects to fall below the minimum should contact the appropriate academic authority as early as possible. The fact that a student attended most classes in a previous semester does not compensate for a shortage in the current course.

Attendance shortages are considered course by course unless a programme regulation expressly provides another method.

### 5. Medical Absence

Medical absence is addressed separately in the Medical Leave Policy. A student who is ill should follow the medical-documentation and leave-submission procedure. Medical documentation by itself does not necessarily convert every missed class into attended sessions.

Where the Medical Leave Policy establishes a different examination-eligibility threshold for an approved medical case, that provision is relevant to the question and must be retrieved alongside the general 75% rule.

### 6. Participation in Official Activities

A student representing the institution in an officially authorised activity may request treatment of the absence under the applicable academic procedure. Approval is not automatic merely because an activity is described as university-related.

The request should normally identify the activity, dates, course affected, and approving authority. The department may require documentary evidence.

### 7. Attendance Disputes

Students should report a suspected attendance-recording error within the period announced by the department. A complaint submitted after the record has been finalised may require additional verification.

The system should distinguish between a rule about correcting an erroneous record and a rule that grants an exemption from the minimum attendance requirement. They are not necessarily the same thing.

### 8. Attendance and Internal Assessment

Where internal assessment includes an attendance-related component, the calculation should follow the course or programme scheme. A student should not infer that satisfying the minimum examination attendance automatically produces full marks for an attendance component.

### 9. Attendance Reports

Periodic attendance reports may be made available through the student portal or department. Students should review reports before the correction period closes.

A student who sees 74% in an attendance report should not be told that the requirement is met merely because the student believes one missed class should not count. The relevant correction or exemption process must be followed.

### 10. Interpretation Rule for the RAG System

The 75% rule in Section 3 is the general rule. It is intentionally placed in the same corpus as a separate medical-leave provision with a lower threshold. A question involving approved medical leave should retrieve both provisions.

The system must not hide the general rule simply because the medical rule is more directly related to the wording of the question. Both provisions are evidence.

### 11. Attendance Example

If a student has 82% attendance in Course A and 73% in Course B, the student satisfies the stated general minimum in Course A but does not satisfy it in Course B. The percentage for one course should not be averaged with another course to create eligibility for the second course.

### 12. Late Arrival

Repeated late arrival may be addressed through course or departmental instructions. This document does not state that every late arrival must be converted into a fixed attendance deduction. Where the corpus does not specify a numerical penalty, the system should not invent one.

### 13. Attendance Waiver Requests

A waiver request should identify the provision under which the student seeks consideration. Submission of a request does not itself grant a waiver.

Where a separate policy gives a committee power to waive an attendance condition, the system should identify that authority rather than stating that a waiver is guaranteed.
