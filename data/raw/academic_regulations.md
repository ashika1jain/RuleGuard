# Project Supplementary Student Regulations
## Academic Regulations

> **Corpus status:** This document is synthetic project material created for the RuleGuard evaluation corpus. It is not an official publication of Medi-Caps University. The authentic placement-policy PDF in this corpus is kept separately and unchanged.

### 1. Purpose and Scope

These supplementary academic regulations define a fictional, controlled set of university rules for testing an evidence-grounded question-answering system. They cover academic registration, course selection, credits, semester progression, examinations, attendance, leave, academic records, and administrative procedures. Where another document in the corpus contains a more specific provision, the system should retrieve both provisions rather than assuming that the longer or newer-looking provision automatically overrides the other one.

### 2. Academic Year and Semester Structure

The academic year is divided into an odd semester and an even semester. The odd semester normally begins in July and concludes after the end-semester assessment period. The even semester normally begins in January and concludes after the corresponding end-semester assessment period. Exact teaching dates may be published separately by the academic office.

Students are responsible for checking the academic calendar, course registration notices, examination notices, and other official communications made available through the university's designated student portal. A student cannot claim that a deadline did not apply merely because the student did not personally read a notice that had been properly published through the designated channel.

### 3. Student Registration

A student must complete semester registration within the period announced by the academic office. Registration normally requires confirmation of the student's programme, semester, selected courses, and any outstanding administrative requirements. A registration request may be held for review where required documents or payments are incomplete.

Students should verify their registered courses after registration closes. Errors in course registration should be reported promptly. A course appearing on a student's timetable does not, by itself, establish that the course has been successfully registered if the student's official academic record does not contain the course.

### 4. Course Selection and Credits

Each programme has an approved curriculum specifying compulsory courses, elective courses, practical components, projects, and other academic requirements. Students may not replace a compulsory course with an unrelated elective merely because the replacement carries the same number of credits.

Where an elective has a stated prerequisite, the prerequisite must normally be satisfied before registration. A department may permit an exception when an approved academic process explicitly authorises it. Such an exception should be recorded in the student's academic record.

A student may not normally register for two courses whose scheduled final examinations occur at the same time. If a timetable conflict is created by an administrative error, the student must report it through the examination or academic office within the announced correction period.

### 5. Academic Progression

Progression from one semester to the next depends on the programme's academic requirements and any applicable backlog provisions. Students are expected to complete required credits in accordance with their programme structure.

A failed course remains part of the student's academic record until it is successfully cleared according to the applicable examination procedure. Passing a later course does not automatically erase a failed course unless the academic regulations explicitly provide for such treatment.

### 6. Academic Records

The university maintains an academic record containing courses, grades, credits, examination outcomes, and other approved academic information. Students should report apparent errors through the designated academic-record correction process.

Requests for correction should identify the course, semester, nature of the suspected error, and supporting evidence. The submission of a correction request does not itself change the official record. A correction takes effect only after it has been approved and recorded by the competent office.

### 7. Academic Integrity

Students are expected to submit their own academic work except where collaboration is expressly permitted. Plagiarism, fabrication of data, impersonation, unauthorised collaboration, and unauthorised assistance during assessments may be treated as academic misconduct.

The presence of another student's work in a submission does not automatically establish who copied from whom. An academic authority may review the available evidence and apply the relevant disciplinary process.

### 8. Project and Practical Work

Where a programme contains project work, the project may include a proposal, progress review, final report, presentation, demonstration, or viva. The department may publish milestones for these components.

A student who misses a project milestone should contact the project supervisor or designated coordinator. Whether a missed milestone can be rescheduled depends on the rules applicable to that component and the reason for the absence.

### 9. Communication and Notices

The student portal is the primary location for academic notices when a notice specifically identifies the portal as the communication channel. Email or another official channel may also be used where stated.

Students should retain copies of important submissions, receipts, approvals, and communications. A screenshot of a personal conversation with another student is not by itself an official academic approval.

### 10. Administrative Review

When a rule requires approval from a department, examination office, academic committee, or other authority, the request must be submitted through the process prescribed for that authority. Approval by an unauthorised individual does not automatically satisfy a rule requiring approval from a specified authority.

Where two provisions appear to apply to the same situation, the provisions should be presented together to the decision-maker rather than silently choosing one. The purpose of this corpus is specifically to test whether a retrieval system can expose such conflicts.

### 11. Exceptional Circumstances

Students may submit requests for consideration in circumstances that are not directly addressed by a standard procedure. An exceptional-circumstances request does not guarantee approval. The competent authority may consider supporting evidence, timing, academic impact, and the relevant regulation.

A general statement that a situation is "urgent" does not itself establish eligibility for an exception.

### 12. Interpretation

These project regulations are intended to be read section by section. Definitions should be interpreted according to the document in which they appear unless a definition is expressly stated to apply across the entire corpus.

A question-answering system should not invent a rule when the corpus is silent. If the retrieved evidence does not establish an answer, the correct classification is NOT_COVERED.
